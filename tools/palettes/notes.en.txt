mozart | Rococo pink, blue and gold. Composing at five and dead at thirty-five, he left more than six hundred works that sound as if they cost no effort at all.
napoleon | The dark blue of the Guard, imperial gold and the sea off Elba. A Corsican artillery officer became emperor, left a civil code and lost at Waterloo in 1815.
beethoven | The colour of cloud before a storm with a little orange from the hearth. After he went deaf he wrote the Ninth Symphony. The original mark is the rhythm that opens the Fifth: three short, one long.
darwin | The logbooks of the Beagle, and the finches and tortoises of the Galapagos. After coming home he thought for more than twenty years before handing the book to a publisher.
lincoln | Union blue, a black broadcloth coat and a log cabin in Kentucky. He held the Union together through the Civil War, signed the Emancipation Proclamation and was shot five days after the main Confederate army surrendered.
dostoevsky | The yellow walls of Petersburg and a candle stub. He stood before a firing squad, was reprieved at the last moment, wore fetters in Siberia for four years and came back to write about the darkest places in people.
tolstoy | The birch woods of Yasnaya Polyana and a peasant's coarse shirt. After two great novels the count tried to give his land away, left home at eighty-two and died at a small railway station.
van-gogh | The yellow of Arles and the cobalt of the night sky, the paint laid on thick. More than two thousand works in ten years, almost none sold in his lifetime, and over six hundred letters to his brother.
tagore | The Bengal monsoon, golden rice fields and boats on the river. He wrote in Bengali, translated himself into English and received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913.
sun-yat-sen | The grey of the suit named after him and the blue of the sea. Trained as a physician, he became provisional president after the 1911 revolution and later drew a plan for a hundred thousand miles of railway.
marie-curie | At night in the shed that served as a laboratory, radium salts gave off a faint blue-green glow in their glass tubes. Two Nobel Prizes, one in physics and one in chemistry; her notebooks are still radioactive.
gandhi | The off-white of hand-spun cloth, the white of salt and a dirt road walked barefoot. He led a march of twenty-four days to the sea to make salt and met an empire with non-cooperation.
einstein | A desk at the patent office, a violin and chalk dust on a blackboard. In 1905, at twenty-six, he published four papers in one year, and space and time have been able to bend ever since.
lu-xun | The black and white of a woodcut with a little dark red. He studied medicine in Sendai, gave it up to write, and wrote short sentences that are cold on the surface and warm underneath.
chaplin | The black, white and silver grey of silent film. After sound arrived he went on making silent pictures for nearly ten years; his hero never says a word and audiences everywhere understand him.
mao-zedong | Born in Hunan; a poet, and the principal founder of the People's Republic of China. The colours come from his 1936 poem on snow: the snowfields of the north and a red sun after the snow.
hemingway | The black of a typewriter, the amber of rum and the sea at Key West. He wrote standing up and cut his sentences shorter and shorter, letting only the tip of the iceberg show.
oppenheimer | Before dawn in the New Mexico desert, a light brighter than the sun. A theoretical physicist who directed the Los Alamos laboratory in wartime and afterwards argued for international control of nuclear weapons.
frida-kahlo | The cobalt of the Blue House, marigold orange and the magenta of a Tehuana dress. After the bus accident at eighteen she spent long periods in bed and painted her pain from a mirror.
turing | A hut at Bletchley Park and an endless paper tape. In 1936 he designed on paper a machine that could only read, write and move left or right; every later computer is a realisation of it.
mandela | The limestone of Robben Island and the green and gold of a patterned shirt. He spent twenty-seven years in prison, was elected president four years after his release, and chose reconciliation.
audrey-hepburn | A little black dress, pearls and a large pair of sunglasses. She went hungry as a girl in occupied Holland, and late in life went to Ethiopia and Somalia as a UNICEF goodwill ambassador.
